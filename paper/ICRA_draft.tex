\begin{abstract}
Bed-bathing is among the most requested and labor-intensive tasks in assistive care, and a
robot that performs it must wipe a person's skin \emph{completely}, \emph{systematically}, and
with \emph{clinically safe} contact forces. Reinforcement learning (RL) yields competent
per-region wiping policies, but a separate network per body part is impractical to deploy, and
the standard contact-counting reward optimizes the wrong objective---clearing targets
aggressively while ignoring coverage and force safety. We contribute: (i) a single
\emph{Context-Conditioned Policy} (CCP) that takes a region identifier and a one-bit care level
and replaces eight per-region specialists, matching or exceeding each and beating a flat
multi-task policy by \best{+33\% targets cleared} (12/12 region$\times$seed pairs, sign test
$p{=}0.0005$) while holding force in the therapeutic band $2$--$2.7\times$ more often;
(ii) a \emph{Comfort reward} that shapes wiping along a self-supervised arm axis with a 2-D
(length$\times$circumference) coverage term; and (iii) a caregiver-selectable
\emph{gentle$\leftrightarrow$thorough} dial: flipping CCP's care bit is a paired same-network
comparison that raises therapeutic-band adherence by \best{+34\%} ($10/12$ pairs, $p{=}0.039$)
at an $8\%$ coverage cost. Across a method ladder, learning dominates a scripted geometric
sweep on every wiping-quality metric---the sweep traces near-perfect geometry yet fails
sustained safe contact---so the reward design decides not how \emph{much} the robot wipes but
how \emph{safely}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Millions of people who cannot bathe independently rely on caregivers for bed-bathing, one of
the most physically demanding and frequently requested activities of daily living. A robot
that assists must move a soft tool across a person's skin, covering each body region fully and
in order while keeping contact force within a gentle, therapeutic range---an inherently
\emph{coverage-and-safety} problem in direct contact with a vulnerable human.

Reinforcement learning can train a competent wiping policy for a single arm region. Two
obstacles keep that from being a system. First, \textbf{deployment}: a robot cannot carry a
library of per-region networks and a switch to select among them. Second, \textbf{objective
mismatch}: the reward shipped with public bed-bathing environments simply counts cleared
targets, which encourages fast dabbing that inflates the count while leaving gaps and spiking
force. A caregiver does the opposite---slow, systematic, gentle, sustained contact.

We address both with one idea: distill per-region, per-reward specialist policies into a
single \emph{Context-Conditioned Policy} (CCP) whose input carries the body region and a
one-bit \emph{care level}, and train it from teachers optimized with a reward built for the
true objective. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{One policy for the whole arm.} A single context-conditioned network replaces
eight region$\times$reward specialists, matches or exceeds each in its own region, and beats a
flat multi-task policy by $+33\%$ targets cleared (positive in all $12$ region$\times$seed
pairs, exact sign test $p{=}0.0005$) while keeping force in-band $2$--$2.7\times$ more often
(Sec.~\ref{sec:flat}).
\item \textbf{The Comfort reward.} A structured reward (Sec.~\ref{sec:reward}) that shapes
wiping along a self-supervised arm axis and adds a 2-D (length$\times$circumference) coverage
term from the same SVD's second principal axis, producing complete, force-safe sweeps.
\item \textbf{A caregiver-selectable dial.} CCP's care bit exposes a runtime
gentle$\leftrightarrow$thorough trade-off (Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). Because it is one network,
flipping the bit is a paired same-network test: the gentle setting raises therapeutic-band
adherence by $+34\%$ ($10/12$ pairs, $p{=}0.039$) at an $8\%$ coverage cost
(Sec.~\ref{sec:dial}).
\end{enumerate}
Across a method ladder (Sec.~\ref{sec:ladder}), learning dominates a non-RL scripted sweep on
every wiping-quality metric, and conditioned distillation dominates flat multi-task RL on both
coverage and safety.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Assistive manipulation in contact with people.} Simulation frameworks such as
Assistive Gym~\cite{erickson2020assistive} established RL benchmarks for bathing, dressing, and
feeding with a human in the loop, where comfort and force are part of the objective. Prior
bed-bathing and dressing work emphasizes safe, personalized contact and force
regulation~\cite{erickson2018dressing,kapusta2019bedside}. Recent dressing policies treat
force limits as an explicit constraint rather than a soft
penalty~\cite{zhang2023forceconstrained}. We follow this line by making therapeutic-band force
a first-class design and evaluation axis, and by exposing safety as a user-selectable behavior.

\textbf{Policy distillation and conditioning.} Distilling one or more RL teachers into a
compact student is well established~\cite{rusu2016distillation}; multi-task and
goal-/context-conditioned policies let one network serve many settings. Our student is
conditioned on both a body region and a care level, so a \emph{single} network both generalizes
across the arm and exposes a behavior dial---and we show the conditioned student regularizes
and can rescue weak teachers, rather than merely compressing them.

\section{Problem Formulation}
A UR5 manipulator holds a soft tool and cleans a target region of a supine human arm in
simulation (PyBullet). Each region carries up to $15$ cleanable \emph{targets}; a target is
\emph{cleared} when the tool passes within $\texttt{clear\_radius}{=}0.022$\,m while in
contact. Episodes last $150$ steps. We model the task as an MDP: the observation
$o\in\mathbb{R}^{90}$ concatenates robot joint state, end-effector pose, tool--skin contact
geometry, and a region/target encoding; the action $a\in\mathbb{R}^{6}$ is a continuous
end-effector command. Safe contact is defined by a \emph{therapeutic force band} of
$[2,12]$\,N: below it the tool grazes without cleaning, above it risks discomfort or injury.
The objective is to maximize coverage (targets cleared, systematic sweep) subject to keeping
contact force in-band---a coverage-and-safety problem, not target collection.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Teacher policies}
We train one PPO policy (RLlib~1.13) per (region, reward) pair. The policy/value network is
$\texttt{fcnet\_hiddens}{=}[100,100]$ with $\tanh$;
$\texttt{train\_batch\_size}{=}19200$, $\texttt{num\_sgd\_iter}{=}50$,
$\texttt{sgd\_minibatch\_size}{=}128$, trained in ${\sim}250$k-step chunks with
best-checkpoint archiving.

\subsection{Reward design}
\label{sec:reward}
We compare two rewards on identical dynamics. The \textbf{Contact-count reward} $\Rcc$ is the
baseline shipped with the public environment:
\begin{equation}
\Rcc = 1.0\,(-d_{\text{arm}}) + 0.01\,(-\lVert a\rVert^2) + 5.0\,c_t,
\label{eq:cc}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\text{arm}}$ is tool--arm distance and $c_t$ the targets cleared at step $t$. It
rewards \emph{how many} targets are cleared but knows nothing of \emph{where} the tool is along
the arm or whether coverage is systematic.

The proposed \textbf{Comfort reward} $\Rcomf$ keeps those base terms and adds four
coverage-and-safety shaping terms (Table~\ref{tab:reward}):
\begin{align}
\Rcomf &= w_d\,r_{\text{dist}} + w_a\,r_{\text{act}} + w_c\,r_{\text{clr}} \nonumber\\
       &\quad + w_s\,r_{\text{sweep}} + w_w\,r_{\text{win}} + w_e\,r_{\text{end}} + w_v\,r_{\text{cov}}.
\label{eq:comf}
\end{align}
A \emph{two-phase distance} $r_{\text{dist}}$ rewards approach while reaching and on-surface
travel while wiping. A \emph{self-supervised sweep axis} term uses the principal axis $u$ of
the target cloud (SVD, refreshed ${\sim}$every $200$ steps) and the tool's normalized sweep
coordinate $s{=}\mathrm{clip}((\langle p{-}\bar p,u\rangle{-}s_{\min})/\mathrm{span},0,1)$;
with $\Delta s_{\text{dir}}{=}\rho(s_t{-}s_{t-1})$ for pass direction $\rho\in\{\pm1\}$,
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{sweep}}=
\begin{cases}
k_f\min(\Delta s_{\text{dir}},\delta), & \Delta s_{\text{dir}}>\varepsilon\\
-k_b\min(-\Delta s_{\text{dir}},\delta), & \Delta s_{\text{dir}}<-\varepsilon\\
0,& \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
active only in contact ($k_f{=}0.5,k_b{=}0.75$), rewarding monotone progress and mildly
penalizing backtracking. A \emph{local-window} term $r_{\text{win}}$ credits contact/clears
near the current $s$; an \emph{evidence-gated end-of-pass bonus} $r_{\text{end}}{=}4.0$ fires
only when a pass reaches an end \emph{and} accumulated real coverage evidence, preventing
farming by sweeping empty space. Finally, the \emph{2-D $(s,w)$ coverage} term uses the
\emph{second} SVD axis---the circumferential (``column'') direction---to form a width
coordinate $w$; the first visit to each cell of a $4{\times}4$ $(s,w)$ grid earns
$r_{\text{cov}}{=}1$ once per episode. The 1-D sweep terms reward progress \emph{along} the
arm; this term is what makes the policy cover \emph{across} it (a grid search over
$w_v\in\{0,0.5,1,2\}$ chose $w_v{=}1$: $6.08$ vs $5.68$ cleared at $w_v{=}0$).

\subsection{Context-Conditioned Policy (CCP)}
CCP is a single diagonal-Gaussian MLP
$\pi_\theta(a\mid o,c){=}\mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta,\mathrm{diag}\,\sigma_\theta^2)$ with a
$(128,128)$ $\tanh$ backbone and heads for $\mu(6)$ and clamped $\log\sigma(6)\in[-5,2]$. Its
\emph{context} $c$ concatenates a region one-hot (4) with a one-bit \emph{care level}
$\kappa\in\{0,1\}$. At deployment the one-hot selects the current body part and $\kappa$
selects behavior: $\kappa{=}0$ (\emph{Gentle}, Comfort-reward behavior) or $\kappa{=}1$
(\emph{Thorough}, Contact-count behavior). One network covers the whole arm and both behaviors.

\subsection{Distillation}
\label{sec:distill}
Each teacher $\pi_r$ is rolled out in its region to record $(o,\mu_r(o),\log\sigma_r(o),c(r))$.
Pooling all regions gives $\mathcal{D}{=}\bigcup_r\mathcal{D}_r$. CCP minimizes a
behavior-cloning term on the mean plus a distribution-matching (KL) term on the full Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
L(\theta)=\lVert\mu_\theta-\mu_T\rVert^2 + \beta\,\mathrm{KL}\!\big(\mathcal{N}_T\,\|\,\mathcal{N}_\theta\big),
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where, per action dimension with log-std $\ell{=}\log\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}\!\big(\mathcal{N}_T\|\mathcal{N}_\theta\big)=\!\sum_{i=1}^{6}\!\Big[\ell_{\theta,i}-\ell_{T,i}
+\tfrac{\sigma_{T,i}^2+(\mu_{T,i}-\mu_{\theta,i})^2}{2\sigma_{\theta,i}^2}-\tfrac12\Big].
\label{eq:kl}
\end{equation}
We minimize the batch mean of Eq.~\ref{eq:loss} with Adam (lr $10^{-3}$, $\beta{=}1$),
matching both the teacher's action mean and its uncertainty (Alg.~\ref{alg:distill}).
Appending the care bit to $c(r)$ yields the dial student; omitting it and pooling all teachers
yields the \emph{flat} student, an irreversible blend.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Context-Conditioned Distillation}
\label{alg:distill}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require teachers $\{\pi_r\}_{r\in R}$; context map $c(r)$; rollout length $N$; weight $\beta$;
optional care values $\{\kappa_r\}$
\State $\mathcal{D}\gets\varnothing$
\For{each region $r\in R$}
  \State roll out $\pi_r$ for $N$ steps; record $(o,\mu_r(o),\log\sigma_r(o),c(r))$
  \State if $\{\kappa_r\}$ given, append $\kappa_r$ to each $c(r)$
  \State $\mathcal{D}\gets\mathcal{D}\cup\mathcal{D}_r$
\EndFor
\State train $\pi_\theta(a\mid o,c)$ by minibatch Adam on the mean of $L$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:loss})
\State \Return single context-conditioned policy $\pi_\theta$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Regions.} Forearm back/front and upper-arm back/front (4).
\textbf{Methods.} Three reward classes---Comfort RL, Contact-count RL, and a non-RL scripted
geometric sweep---each as a per-region \emph{specialist} and a distilled \emph{student}; the
proposed \textbf{CCP} distilled from all eight RL specialists and evaluated at both care
settings; and a \textbf{flat} multi-task PPO baseline (Comfort reward, all regions, no
conditioning). \textbf{Protocol.} Identical metric reducers, $200$ episodes/region ($100$ for
the deterministic scripted controller), force band $[2,12]$\,N, and \textbf{3 training seeds}
per learned method. For significance we use exact sign tests over the $12$ region$\times$seed
pairs plus a conservative per-seed paired $t$-test---the seed, not the episode, is the
statistical unit. Every number is emitted by \texttt{analyze\_final.py} from the metrics CSV.

\textbf{Metrics.} Per step $t$ an episode yields force $f_t$, an in-contact flag,
sweep coordinate $s_t$, per-step clears $k_t$; $\mathcal{C}{=}\{t:\text{contact}\}$ and $T$ the
episode length. Table~\ref{tab:metrics} defines the reported set. Two families are excluded
from cross-method comparison: \emph{pass completions} (counts the Comfort reward's own gate---one
method's machinery) and \emph{mean/p95 force} (reward \emph{not touching}: the scripted sweep
has the lowest mean force because it grazes), so force safety is reported as in-band fraction.

\subsection{A caregiver-selectable gentle$\leftrightarrow$thorough dial}
\label{sec:dial}
CCP spans the trade-off at deployment time. Averaged over 4 regions $\times$ 3 seeds: Thorough
mode $6.80$ cleared / $0.156$ in-band; Gentle mode $6.17$ cleared / $0.198$ in-band, with the
ordering consistent on all four regions (Table~\ref{tab:main}: Gentle is gentler in $4/4$
regions, Thorough clears $\geq$ in $4/4$). Because CCP is \emph{one}
network, flipping $\kappa$ is a perfectly paired comparison---same weights, seeds, regions;
only the care bit differs. Switching to Gentle raises \best{in-band force fraction by $+34\%$}
(better in $10/12$ region$\times$seed pairs, exact sign test $p{=}0.039$) at an $8\%$ coverage
cost ($3/12$, n.s.). On composite \emph{safe clears} (cleared$\times$in-band), the
Comfort-distilled behavior leads by $+28\%$. The raw clear-count ordering is reversed
(Thorough clears more): the reward design does not make wiping more \emph{productive}, it makes
it \emph{safer}, and the dial hands that choice to the caregiver.

\subsection{CCP beats the flat multi-task baseline}
\label{sec:flat}
In Thorough mode CCP clears \best{6.80} vs \best{5.11} for the flat baseline---\best{$+33\%$},
positive in \best{$12/12$ region$\times$seed pairs} (exact sign test $p{=}0.0005$), consistent
per seed ($6.43/6.81/7.14$ vs $5.26/5.37/4.69$). The Comfort-distilled student alone gains
$+1.16$ clears/seed (per-seed paired $t{=}3.76$, $n{=}3$). Conditioning also buys safety: every
conditioned student keeps force in-band $2$--$2.7\times$ more than the flat baseline
($0.153$--$0.198$ vs $0.074$). One network replaces four specialists and beats the
unconditioned alternative on both axes (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}).

\subsection{Distillation $\geq$ specialists---and rescues weak teachers}
CCP-family students match specialists where those are strong and repair them where weak
(cleared/15): Comfort teacher$\to$student $4.99\to\best{6.23}$ on forearm front and
$5.19\to\best{5.96}$ on upper-arm front; Contact-count $4.95\to\best{5.69}$ on upper-arm front.
No region regresses by more than $0.3$. Grand means: students $6.17$--$6.80$ vs specialists
$5.79$--$6.38$---pooling regularizes rather than merely compressing.

\subsection{Ablating the design: learning and conditioning both matter}
\label{sec:ladder}
The rows of Table~\ref{tab:main} form an ablation of the full method. Removing the coverage
term ($w_v{=}0$) drops cleared from $6.08$ to $5.68$. Removing region conditioning yields the
\emph{flat} baseline ($5.11$ / $0.074$). Removing learning entirely yields the \emph{scripted}
sweep ($4.56$ / $0.039$). Removing the care bit yields the single-family students, which cannot
switch behavior. The result is a monotone ladder: scripted $<$ flat $<$ specialists
($5.79$--$6.38$) $<$ CCP students ($6.17$--$6.80$). The scripted controller is not merely less
productive---it fails the wiping-\emph{quality} battery, in contact only $25$--$41\%$ of steps
(vs $51$--$74\%$ for CCP) with in-band force collapsing to $0.02$ on the upper arm---yet its
near-perfect clear spatial order ($0.997$) shows it traces textbook geometry. Raw clear-count
and geometric orderliness are mirages of wiping; sustained safe contact had to be learned.
Crucially, \textbf{distillation cannot manufacture it}: cloning the scripted controller into a
student adds a little coverage ($4.56\to5.05$) but leaves in-band force at ${\sim}0.04$ vs
${\sim}0.19$ for RL students. Distillation \emph{amplifies} what a teacher already does; the
generalization is a property of the learned behavior, not of the distillation mechanism.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Reachability.} One angular column of each \emph{back} region faces the bed---the
supine limb's support surface. Per-target IK over a dense orientation grid, dynamic
clearability under three controllers, and base-/posture-pose sweeps all show no configuration
of this environment can wipe it (the humanoid lacks a forearm-pronation DOF). This caps
targets-cleared near $10/15$ on back regions and keeps \texttt{task\_success} ($69\%$
threshold) near zero for \emph{all} methods; the support-facing surface requires physically
re-positioning the limb, a caregiver action assigned to the pipeline's classical phases.
\textbf{Force spikes.} Peak contact forces reach $47$--$68$\,N for all methods; bounding
worst-case force (force-rate shaping or a controller-level clamp) is required before hardware.
\textbf{Statistics.} Sign tests over $12$ pairs are exact, but per-seed $n{=}3$; gaps quoted
without a $p$-value are directional. \textbf{Scope.} Results are simulation-only; sim-to-real
transfer is future work.

\section{Conclusion}
A single Context-Conditioned Policy, conditioned on body region and a one-bit care level, wipes
all four arm regions, beats a flat multi-task baseline in every region$\times$seed pair
($+33\%$, $p{=}0.0005$), and exposes a caregiver-selectable gentle$\leftrightarrow$thorough
dial whose gentle end raises therapeutic-band adherence by $34\%$ in a paired same-network test
($p{=}0.039$). Learning dominates scripting on every wiping-quality metric, and conditioned
distillation dominates flat multi-task RL on both coverage and safety. The reward design
decides not how \emph{much} a robot wipes but how \emph{safely}; distillation turns that choice
into a runtime-switchable property of one deployable network. Bounding peak force and
sim-to-real transfer are the next steps toward hardware trials.
