# Why the third column of the forearm is not wipeable — geometric reachability analysis

*Prepared for review. Region studied: `forearm_back`. All results from the PyBullet sim
(UR5 + supine humanoid), validated on the real environment.*

## 1. Summary (one paragraph)

Each arm region is discretised into ~15 target points wrapped around the limb surface. On
the forearm, these form **3 angular columns × 5 length-rows**. Across every experiment, the
robot cleans two of the three columns and leaves the third (the one at local azimuth ≈ +98°)
essentially untouched. We show this is **not a policy or reward failure** but a **physical
reachability limit**: that column is the **bed-facing underside of the supine forearm**, and
the humanoid model has **no forearm pronation/supination degree of freedom**, so the surface
cannot be rotated to face the tool. No robot base pose, no arm posture, and no combined
re-pose exposes it beyond ~20%. It is unwipeable without physically lifting/turning the limb —
a caregiver action outside the current wiping controller's scope.

## 2. Target geometry — what "columns" are

Targets are generated by `util.capsule_points` around the limb capsule
(`WipingEnv.generate_targets`). For the forearm (length 0.164 m, radius 0.028 m, spacing
0.028 m) this yields **5 rings along the length × 6 points around the circumference**. A
"region" is one half of that surface (median split), giving **≈ 3 angular columns × 5 rows
≈ 15 targets**. The three columns sit at local azimuths **{+98°, +155°, +213°}** — a ~115°
arc of the circumference. The robot reliably clears the +155° and +213° columns; +98° is the
one left behind.

## 3. The reasoning chain — how we concluded (and where naïve IK misled us)

We deliberately cross-checked **four independent lines of evidence**, because the first
(static inverse kinematics) turned out to be unreliable.

### 3.1 Per-target inverse kinematics (necessary, but *not sufficient*)

The environment's own feasibility test only solves IK to the region **centroid** and then
accepts *all* 15 targets — it never checks targets individually. We therefore wrote a
per-target probe (`reachability_probe.py`): for each target we solve
`calculateInverseKinematics` for an end-effector pose that places the tool tip within
`clear_radius`, over a **dense grid of tool orientations (±60° about two axes)**, and accept
the target only if some orientation is **in joint-limits, collision-free, and lands the tip
within clear_radius** (collision via the env's own `robot_in_collision`).

**Important caveat we discovered:** static IK reachability was **anti-predictive** here. It
reported the +98° column as ≥90% "reachable" and a *different* column (−147°) as only 8%
"reachable" — yet in behaviour the −147° column is cleared ~66% and +98° is cleared **0%**.
Static "can the tip be placed near the point in *some* static pose" does **not** predict "can
the tool make sustained in-band contact and clear it during wiping." **So we did not rely on
IK reachability alone** — this is the key methodological correction.

### 3.2 Dynamic clearability — the faithful test (three independent controllers agree)

We instead measured **dynamic clearability**: drive the tool onto each column with the real
environment physics and its own contact/clear detection, and record what actually clears.
Three completely different controllers were run:

| Controller | col @ +98° cleared | cols @ +155° / +213° |
|---|---|---|
| Trained RL policy (structured reward) | **0%** | 55–65% |
| Scripted single-lane sweep | **0%** | 58–62% |
| Scripted **column-aware raster** (explicitly targets every column) | **0%** | 60–68% |

Three independent controllers — including one that *explicitly drives to each column* —
clearing +98° at exactly 0% is strong convergent evidence that the target cannot be contacted,
i.e. a **geometry limit, not a controller/reward limit**.

### 3.3 Configuration sweep — can a different robot approach reach it?

We physically repositioned the UR5 base (`config_coverage.py`: default +x, mirror −x, side
+y) and re-tested clearability on the same targets. **+98° stayed 0% in every base pose.**
Moving the robot rotates the *approach direction* but cannot reach a surface pressed against
the bed on the far side of the limb.

### 3.4 Surface geometry + kinematics — why no re-pose fixes it

We computed each column's **outward surface normal** in world coordinates. The +155°/+213°
columns face **up / toward the robot**; the **+98° column's normal faces down (toward the
bed)** — it is the underside the arm rests on.

To expose it one would have to **roll the forearm about its own long axis**. We identified the
candidate DOF geometrically (`pose_feasibility.py`, Phase 1: `right_shoulder_roll` tilts the
forearm axis least) and swept it — plus shoulder yaw/pitch and elbow — through ±90°
(`pose_combo.py`). **No single re-pose, and no combined lift+roll+yaw posture, exposed +98°
above 20%** (best: shoulder pitch −90° + roll +60° + yaw −30° → 20%).

The root cause is kinematic and definitive: **the humanoid URDF has no forearm
pronation/supination joint.** The right arm chain is
`shoulder_yaw → shoulder_pitch → shoulder_roll → elbow(flexion)`; the forearm targets attach
to the `right_elbow` link, whose orientation is fully determined by the shoulder and a 1-DOF
elbow. There is **no joint that spins the forearm surface about its own axis**, so the
bed-facing band cannot be turned to face the tool. Shoulder rolls *swing* the whole arm (and
tend to drive it into the bed) rather than *rotating* the forearm surface — which is exactly
what the sweep showed.

## 4. Conclusion

The +98° column is the **bed-occluded underside of the supine forearm**. It is unreachable by:
- any tool orientation (dense IK grid), **and**
- any robot base station (base sweep), **and**
- any arm posture achievable in this model (pose sweep) — because the model has **no forearm
  roll DOF**.

Cleaning it would require **physically lifting or turning the limb** — a caregiver
re-positioning action, i.e. Phase-1/2 of the deployment pipeline, *outside* the wiping
controller's remit. This is a realistic, clinically-faithful limitation, not a defect of the
learned policy.

## 5. Consequences (why this matters beyond one column)

1. **It explains the long-standing `task_success ≈ 0`.** Success needs
   `cleared ≥ 0.69 × 15 ≈ 11`. With ~5 targets (one column) physically unclearable, the ceiling
   is ~10 → success is *mathematically impossible*. This is the "6.9 → 10.4 structural gap"
   that earlier tuning (entropy, curriculum, budget) could never close — because it was
   grading against unreachable targets.
2. **The fix — reachable-arc scoping.** We drop the robot-averted band (the target column whose
   outward normal points away from the robot), scoping each region to its wipeable ~120° arc.
   This is applied *uniformly to every method*, makes `task_success` meaningful again
   (≈ 0 → ≈ 0.3 with the same policy), and lets us report honest complete-coverage of the
   accessible surface. Full-circumference coverage is scoped as future work requiring limb
   re-positioning.
